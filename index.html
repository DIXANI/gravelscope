How to calculate how much gravel you need

Gravel is usually measured by volume for the project area and sold either by weight, bulk volume or bags. GravelScope calculates the project volume first, then uses the selected bulk density to estimate weight.

Measure the project area.Enter the length and width, circle diameter, or an area you already know.
Choose the finished gravel depth.Use the depth your project requires rather than guessing from the total material pile.
Select the material and allowance.Density converts volume into weight. Add an allowance for waste, settlement or compaction when appropriate.
Formula: volume = area × depth. Estimated weight = adjusted volume × bulk density. Gravel density can vary with stone size, moisture and grading, so use your supplier's density whenever they provide one.

What gravel depth should you use?

The correct depth depends on the material and the job. Decorative landscape gravel may use a relatively shallow layer, while driveways and load-bearing bases often need deeper, compacted layers and sometimes multiple materials.

These are general planning ranges, not engineering specifications. Local soil conditions, drainage and load requirements can change the correct depth.

Why tons and cubic yards can differ

A cubic yard measures volume; a ton measures weight. The same cubic-yard volume can weigh differently depending on the gravel type, particle size, moisture and how tightly the material is packed. That is why GravelScope shows both volume and estimated weight instead of using one fixed “yards-to-tons” conversion for every material.

Should you add extra gravel?

Often, yes. Real projects can lose material to uneven ground, settlement, compaction, spreading and measurement error. The calculator starts with a 10% allowance, but you can change it to match your project. For a large or structural job, ask the supplier or contractor what allowance they recommend.

Worked example

Suppose a rectangular area is 20 ft long and 10 ft wide with a 2 in gravel layer. The base volume is about 33.3 cubic feet. Adding a 10% allowance gives about 36.7 cubic feet, or about 1.36 cubic yards. The weight then depends on the selected bulk density.

This is why changing the gravel type in the calculator can change the tonnage without changing the project dimensions.

Gravel calculator FAQ

How accurate is this gravel calculator?

The geometry is calculated directly from the dimensions you enter. Weight is an estimate because bulk density varies between materials, suppliers, moisture levels and gradations. For ordering, replace the preset density with supplier data when possible.

How do I calculate gravel for a driveway?

Measure the driveway length and width, enter the planned layer depth, then select the closest material type. For driveways, the correct base structure and compaction depend on soil, drainage and vehicle load, so use local contractor or engineering guidance for the required layers.

How many bags of gravel do I need?

Enter the bag size shown by your supplier. GravelScope divides the estimated project weight by the bag weight and rounds up to the next whole bag.

Why should I add a waste or compaction allowance?

An allowance helps account for uneven ground, spreading loss, settlement, compaction and measurement error. It is adjustable because the appropriate percentage depends on the job.

Is GravelScope free?

Yes. The calculator is free to use and does not require an account.
