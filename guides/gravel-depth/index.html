Depth guide

How Deep Should Gravel Be?

Choose a realistic depth before calculating material. The right number depends on traffic, stone size, ground conditions and the job the layer must do.

Planning guideUpdated Oct 2026

Depth depends on the job

There is no single gravel depth that works for every project. A decorative garden strip carries almost no load, while a driveway must resist repeated vehicle traffic and usually depends on the condition of the ground below it. The calculator therefore treats depth as a user input rather than assuming one universal value.

For planning, decorative gravel is often considered in a relatively shallow layer, while walking surfaces and driveway layers usually need more depth. The stone size matters too: a layer that is thinner than the stone itself will not cover evenly, and larger aggregate generally needs enough depth to interlock rather than sit as a single loose layer.

Typical planning ranges

UseStarting rangeKey consideration
Decorative bedsAbout 2–3 inStone size, weed control and appearance
Garden pathsAbout 2–4 inFoot traffic, edging and base condition
Driveway surfaceAbout 3–4+ inVehicle load, drainage and compaction
Structural/base layersProject-specificSubgrade, local practice and engineering requirements

These are planning ranges, not construction specifications. A new driveway on soft soil may need a prepared sub-base and several compacted layers rather than one thick layer of surface gravel.

Why compaction changes the order quantity

Loose aggregate settles as particles rearrange and voids reduce. If your finished design requires a compacted depth, ordering exactly the loose geometric volume may leave you short. This is one reason GravelScope includes an optional extra allowance.

Do not treat the allowance as a substitute for a proper layer design. For larger driveways, drainage work, retaining areas or load-bearing construction, confirm the required aggregate type, lift thickness and compaction method with a local supplier or qualified professional.

Measure depth in several places

Real sites are rarely perfectly level. If the ground varies, take several depth measurements or divide the project into simpler zones. A small low area across a large driveway can consume much more material than expected. The most useful input is the average finished material depth after realistic preparation, not the deepest point on the site.

Calculator tip: run the estimate twice using a lower and higher reasonable depth. The difference shows how sensitive your order quantity is to that assumption.
