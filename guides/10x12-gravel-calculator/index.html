How to Calculate Gravel for a 10x12 Area

The calculation follows three steps: find the area, convert depth to feet, multiply to get volume, then convert to your preferred unit.

1
Calculate area: 10 ft × 12 ft = 144 square feet
2
Convert depth to feet: Divide inches by 12. For 4 inches: 4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 ft
3
Multiply area × depth: 144 × 0.333 = 48 cubic feet
4
Convert to cubic yards: 48 ÷ 27 = 1.78 cubic yards
5
Convert to tons: 1.78 × 1,600 ÷ 2,000 = 1.42 tons (using 1,600 lb/yd³ density)

For metric users: 3m × 3.6m at 10cm depth = 10.8 cubic meters = approximately 17.3 tons of general gravel.

Worked Example: 10x12 Driveway Section

Here is a complete worked example for a typical driveway application:

  1. Area: 10 ft × 12 ft = 144 sq ft
  2. Target finished depth: 4 inches (standard for light vehicle traffic)
  3. Compaction factor: 15% settlement expected
  4. Loose depth to spread: 4 ÷ 0.85 = 4.7 inches (round to 5 inches)
  5. Volume: 144 × (5/12) = 60 cubic feet = 2.22 cubic yards
  6. Weight (general gravel): 2.22 × 1,600 = 3,552 lb = 1.78 tons
  7. Add 10% allowance for waste: 1.78 × 1.10 = 1.96 tons
  8. Final order: approximately 2.0 tons or 2.4 cubic yards

This example shows why simply calculating the geometric volume is not enough. The compaction factor and waste allowance both increase the order quantity above the bare minimum.

Tips for Accurate 10x12 Estimation

  • Measure twice. Verify both dimensions before calculating. A 10x12 area might actually be 9.5x12.5 due to irregular boundaries, which changes the area by about 10%.
  • Account for compaction. If you need a finished depth of 4 inches, order for approximately 4.5-5 inches loose to allow for 10-20% settlement during compaction.
  • Check ground conditions. Soft or uneven ground may require additional material to fill low spots after the base layer is placed.
  • Add a waste allowance. A 5-10% overage covers spillage, edge trimming and minor measurement errors without significantly increasing cost.
  • Consider delivery minimums. Some suppliers have minimum order quantities that may exceed your calculated need. Plan accordingly.
  • Compare supplier quotes. Get prices from at least 2-3 suppliers and compare on the same unit basis (tons vs cubic yards).

Common Questions

How much gravel for a 10x12 area?

For a 10x12 area at 4 inches deep with general gravel, you need approximately 1.48 cubic yards or 2.37 tons. This covers a 144 square foot area, commonly used for patios, driveways, and garden pathways.

How many cubic yards of gravel for 10x12 at 3 inches deep?

For a 10x12 area at 3 inches deep, you need 1.11 cubic yards of gravel. Calculation: (10 × 12 × 3/12) ÷ 27 = 1.11 cubic yards.

How many tons of gravel for a 10x12 driveway?

For a 10x12 driveway section at 4 inches deep with general gravel (1,600 lb/yd³), you need approximately 1.48 tons. Add 10% for compaction and waste: 1.63 tons total.

What depth of gravel is best for a 10x12 patio?

For a 10x12 patio, 4-6 inches of compacted gravel is recommended for durability. This translates to 1.11-1.67 cubic yards of loose gravel, or about 1.8-2.7 tons depending on stone type.

How many bags of gravel for a 10x12 area?

For a 10x12 area at 4 inches deep, you need approximately 48 bags of 50lb gravel. At 6 inches deep, you need about 72 bags. Each 50lb bag provides roughly 0.5 cubic feet of coverage.
