Gravel Density: Tons vs Cubic Yards

Volume tells you how much space the gravel fills. Density is what turns that volume into an estimated weight.

Planning guideUpdated Oct 2026

Volume and weight are different measurements

Cubic yards and cubic feet describe how much space the gravel occupies. Tons and kilograms describe weight. Suppliers may sell by either measure, which is why a project can be calculated correctly by volume but still need a density estimate before it can be converted to tons.

The basic relationship is simple: estimated weight equals volume multiplied by bulk density. The difficulty is that bulk density is not identical for every aggregate.

Why gravel density varies

Stone type, particle size, grading, moisture and the amount of empty space between particles all affect bulk density. Crushed stone with a broad mix of particle sizes can pack differently from rounded river rock or clean, uniformly sized gravel. Wet material can also weigh more than dry material.

This is why a generic calculator density is best treated as a planning estimate. If a quarry, landscape yard or supplier publishes a weight per cubic yard or bulk density for the exact product you plan to buy, use that figure instead.

Converting cubic yards to tons

First calculate the project volume from area and depth. Then convert that volume to the unit used by the density value. GravelScope performs this conversion automatically for its preset materials and lets you enter a custom density when you have better supplier data.

Example: if two products occupy the same 2 cubic yards but one has a higher bulk density, the heavier product will require more tons even though the project volume is unchanged.

Which number should you order by?

Follow the supplier's sales unit. If the supplier sells bulk material by ton, use the estimated weight and round according to their ordering rules. If the supplier sells by cubic yard, use the adjusted volume. For bagged material, use the bag count based on the published bag weight or coverage.

Delivery trucks also have payload limits, so a large project may require multiple loads even when the total volume fits your site. Delivery planning is separate from the material calculation and should be confirmed with the supplier.
