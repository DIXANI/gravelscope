How to plan a gravel project

A good gravel estimate starts with accurate measurements. Measure the length and width of the area, choose a realistic finished depth, and then use the GravelScope calculator to convert that volume into cubic yards, tons, kilograms or bags. For irregular areas, divide the project into smaller rectangles or sections, calculate each one separately, and add the results together.

Depth matters as much as surface area. A decorative garden bed may need a different depth from a pedestrian path or driveway, while a structural base can require several layers. If you are unsure, start with the gravel depth guide and check any local installation requirements before ordering.

Gravel weight and density

Suppliers may sell gravel by volume or by weight, so it helps to understand the difference between cubic yards and tons. The same volume can weigh more or less depending on stone type, grading, moisture and compaction. GravelScope uses practical density assumptions for planning, but a supplier's stated density is the better value when one is available.

Use the gravel density and tons-to-cubic-yards guide to understand the conversion before comparing quotes or arranging delivery.

Cost, delivery and ordering allowance

The material price is only part of a gravel project budget. Delivery charges, minimum order quantities, access for a truck, base preparation, edging, geotextile fabric and labour can all change the final cost. The gravel cost guide explains how to separate these costs so that a low price per ton does not hide a more expensive overall project.

It can also be sensible to allow a little extra material for settlement, compaction, uneven ground, measurement error or future topping up. Avoid applying a large percentage automatically. Use the extra gravel allowance guide to decide whether your project genuinely needs an allowance.

Choose the right gravel type

Pea gravel, crushed stone, decorative gravel and compactable sub-base materials behave differently. Rounded gravel is often chosen for appearance and drainage, while angular crushed materials can interlock more effectively under traffic. Size also affects comfort, stability, drainage and the finished look.

Before ordering, compare your intended use with the gravel types and sizes guide. For driveways or structural work, follow local construction guidance and your supplier's recommendations rather than relying on a calculator alone.

Common project-size examples

If you want a quick worked example, the 10x10 gravel calculator guide and 10x12 gravel calculator guide show how area and depth change the required volume, weight and bag count. These examples are useful for patios, garden areas, shed surrounds and other small projects, but the same calculation method can be applied to larger areas.

When you are ready, return to the GravelScope gravel calculator, enter your measurements and selected depth, then compare the estimate with your local supplier's product density, bag size and delivery requirements.
