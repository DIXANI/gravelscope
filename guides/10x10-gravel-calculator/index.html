How to Calculate Gravel for a 10x10 Area

The calculation follows five steps: find the area, convert depth to feet, multiply to get volume, then convert that volume to cubic yards and to tons.

1
Calculate area: 10 ft × 10 ft = 100 square feet
2
Convert depth to feet: Divide inches by 12. For 4 inches: 4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 ft
3
Multiply area × depth: 100 × 0.333 = 33.3 cubic feet
4
Convert to cubic yards: 33.3 ÷ 27 = 1.23 cubic yards
5
Convert to tons: 1.23 × 1,600 ÷ 2,000 = 0.99 tons (using 1,600 lb/yd³ density)

For metric users: 10m × 10m at 10cm depth = 100 × 0.1 = 10 cubic meters = approximately 9.5 tonnes of general gravel (using 1,600 lb/yd³, which is about 949 kg/m³).

Worked Example: 10x10 Driveway Section

Here is a complete worked example for a typical driveway application:

  1. Area: 10 ft × 10 ft = 100 sq ft
  2. Target finished depth: 4 inches (standard for light vehicle traffic)
  3. Compaction factor: 15% settlement expected
  4. Loose depth to spread: 4 ÷ 0.85 = 4.7 inches (round to 5 inches)
  5. Volume: 100 × (5/12) = 41.7 cubic feet = 1.54 cubic yards
  6. Weight (general gravel): 1.54 × 1,600 = 2,464 lb = 1.23 tons
  7. Add 10% allowance for waste: 1.23 × 1.10 = 1.35 tons
  8. Final order: approximately 1.4 tons or 1.7 cubic yards

This example shows why simply calculating the geometric volume is not enough. The compaction factor and waste allowance both increase the order quantity above the bare minimum.

Tips for Accurate 10x10 Estimation

  • Measure twice. Verify both dimensions before calculating. A 10x10 area might actually be 9.5x10.5 due to irregular boundaries, which changes the area by about 10%.
  • Account for compaction. If you need a finished depth of 4 inches, order for approximately 4.5-5 inches loose to allow for 10-20% settlement during compaction.
  • Check ground conditions. Soft or uneven ground may require additional material to fill low spots after the base layer is placed.
  • Add a waste allowance. A 5-10% overage covers spillage, edge trimming and minor measurement errors without significantly increasing cost.
  • Consider delivery minimums. Some suppliers have minimum order quantities that may exceed your calculated need. Plan accordingly.

Common Questions

How much gravel do I need for a 10x10 area?

For a 10x10 area at 2 inches deep, you need about 0.62 cubic yards (16.7 cubic feet) of gravel. At 4 inches deep, you need about 1.23 cubic yards. The exact amount depends on the depth and gravel type you choose.

How many tons of gravel for a 10x10 patio?

For a 10x10 patio at 4 inches deep with general gravel (1,600 lb/yd³), you need approximately 0.99 tons. This converts to about 1,980 lbs or roughly 40 bags of 50lb gravel.

How many cubic yards of gravel for 10x10 at 3 inches deep?

For a 10x10 area at 3 inches deep, you need 0.93 cubic yards of gravel. This is calculated as: (10 × 10 × 3/12) ÷ 27 = 0.93 cubic yards.

What depth of gravel is best for a 10x10 driveway?

For a 10x10 driveway section, 4-6 inches of compacted gravel is recommended. This translates to approximately 1.23-1.85 cubic yards of gravel, or about 0.99-1.48 tons at 1,600 lb/yd³ depending on the stone type.

How many bags of gravel for a 10x10 area?

For a 10x10 area at 2 inches deep, you need approximately 20 bags of 50lb gravel. At 4 inches deep, you need about 40 bags. Bag counts are based on weight: at 1,600 lb/yd³ a 4-inch layer weighs about 1,975 lb, and 1,975 ÷ 50 = 40 bags. Each 50lb bag holds roughly 0.5 cubic feet, and 33.3 cubic feet ÷ 0.5 = 67 bags by volume, so the weight-based count is lower because each bag is heavier than 25 lb per half cubic foot at this density.
